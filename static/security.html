TABLE GUIDE

Fair play & security

Your browser gets a seat at the table. The server holds the deck.

What that means for players

Before each hand, the server shuffles a fresh 52-card deck. It deals from that deck in order. Refreshing the page, changing the buttons, or watching the network requests won’t let you choose the next card or see an opponent’s folded cards.

You see your own cards. When betting is over in a called all-in, the remaining live hands turn face up; cards that players folded stay private. The percentages beside exposed hands estimate who will win. They do not influence the deal. The host gets the same card view as any other player. Test bots use their own cards and public table information to choose legal moves. Their strategy never receives opponents’ hole cards or the undealt deck.

How the deal works

The Python engine shuffles with random.SystemRandom, which draws randomness from the operating system. The deck order stays on the server. The odds calculator separately counts or samples possible boards from visible cards; it does not look at the undealt deck.

What a modified client can do

A client may request a fold, call, check, bet or raise. The server checks the signed-in player, whose turn it is, the hand and turn version, the permitted amount and available chips. It calculates pots and awards itself. A stale request cannot act on the next turn. Player-specific responses omit the deck and opponents’ hidden cards.

Accounts and access

Use your own username and password. The server stores a salted PBKDF2-SHA256 password verifier; it does not store your password as text. The public connection uses HTTPS. The app checks the request origin and requires both a session cookie and a separate browser-held proof; changes also require a CSRF token. Treat your signed-in device as you would your seat.

What this cannot guarantee

No online poker room can promise that nobody will cheat. Players can share cards outside the app, create multiple accounts, or share a password. The service operator controls the code and database. There is no public shuffle proof or independent fairness certification. Buy-ins and payouts are tracked in dollars. The app does not collect or transfer money.
